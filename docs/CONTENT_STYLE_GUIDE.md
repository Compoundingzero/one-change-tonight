# Content style guide

Lead with the direct answer; state uncertainty in the same first section. Use familiar words, one idea per sentence, and observable distinctions: room, bed, timing, location, moisture, partner, and response to a previous change. “Personal heat” is a non-diagnostic location/timing description.

Say “may,” “suggests testing,” and “cannot establish” when evidence is incomplete. Never convert an environmental pattern into a cause. Never imply menopause from age, night sweats, or a hot/cold sequence. Never use cure, prevention, treatment, clinical proof, guaranteed result, expert approval, hands-on test, or medical review unless the exact statement is true, sourced, and approved.

Every query-led page includes: direct answer; what fits/does not fit; distinguishing observations; one reversible experiment; what not to buy yet; safety boundary; next-question links; sources and review date. Avoid duplicated introductions, rhetorical fear, partner blame, promotional adjectives, fabricated authority, and keyword variations that add no distinct decision.

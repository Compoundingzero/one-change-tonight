# UX principles

1. Put the tired person’s next action first.
2. Show one question and one dominant action per step.
3. Keep uncertainty and safety visible, not hidden behind disclosure.
4. Prefer native controls, reversible actions, 44-pixel targets, plain language, and stable focus.
5. Treat room, bed, body, and partner as observations—not diagnoses or blame.
6. Make the low-cost learning step easier than purchasing.
7. Keep public answers self-contained and assessment state private.
8. Use calm hierarchy and whitespace as structure; avoid card soup, decorative motion, remote fonts, and borrowed trade dress.

# Transferable pattern library

Patterns are synthesized across references; none is permission to reproduce a site’s visual identity.

## 1. Action before explanation

Cleanup.pictures, Squoosh and TinyPNG place the usable input before long content. IT-Tools individual routes and creative editors likewise let the user act quickly.

Translation: show the first assessment question on the homepage after one promise, one trust line and a concise medical boundary. Supporting evidence follows. This fits a tired user who came from search and already knows the problem.

## 2. Output as the visual center

Shots, Ray, Carbon, Squoosh and Excalidraw reserve their largest area for the artifact being made. Controls support rather than visually replace it.

Translation: treat “One Change Tonight” as the output canvas. Give it the strongest border, clearest title and most useful action sequence. The pattern label and deeper explanation remain secondary.

## 3. Guided assembly from small units

readme.so builds a document from named sections; Transform.tools makes input/output causality explicit; IT-Tools gives each utility a bounded job.

Translation: one question per screen, large answer cards and visible clues. The user builds a Room–Bed–Body–Partner picture without seeing a medical form or score.

## 4. Progressive disclosure

Ray and Squoosh show a useful default before advanced settings; Cleanup reveals brush/result controls after input; Shots groups customization in inspectors.

Translation: first deliver a pattern and one experiment. Only then show deeper evidence, three-night tracking, mechanism comparisons and appointment notes. Medical boundaries, stop conditions and Do Not Buy Yet are never hidden.

## 5. Privacy at the moment of risk

Squoosh states that images stay on the device; Excalidraw explains browser storage and its limits before work. TinyPNG demonstrates the opposite lesson: a material 48-hour retention fact is too far from upload.

Translation: “No account. No name. Your answers stay in this browser.” Place it before assessment, near morning check-in and on the privacy page. Explain deletion and browser-storage limitations honestly.

## 6. Visible, reversible response

Cleanup visibly marks a selection, transforms the image and reveals download; Squoosh changes from processing to a size/savings result; Excalidraw and Shots expose undo/restart.

Translation: selected answers gain radio, border and surface changes; Continue enables; progress changes; Back preserves state; result clues cite chosen observations; morning check-ins update the experiment immediately; Start over and delete remain available.

## 7. Decision economy

The best first-run states offer one obvious action even when the full product is complex. Their failures are instructive: Carbon’s mobile toolbar and Transform’s directory crowd the task.

Translation: one primary action per question, no navigation taxonomy during assessment, no product-mechanism choice before a result, no sharing pressure and no gamification.

## 8. Tool plus static answer shell

Cleanup, Canva and TinyPNG place substantial content after the tool; IT-Tools and Transform use direct routes; sparse apps such as Ray and Carbon show why a health-adjacent product needs more context than a creative utility.

Translation: render H1, promise, privacy, boundaries, framework, evidence links and direct article answers as HTML. Hydrate only the assessment and tracker. Search pages answer the query first and enter the relevant generic tool context without encoding answers in the URL.

## 9. Memorability through a system, not a mascot

Squoosh and TinyPNG are memorable through playful assets; Excalidraw through its canvas language; creative editors through recognizable outputs. Those devices are inappropriate to copy.

Translation: memory comes from the name, one shifted line in a stable mark, the four-part Room–Bed–Body–Partner language, the one-change loop and a consistent experiment-card structure.
